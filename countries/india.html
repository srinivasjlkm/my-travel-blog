Ladakh

3 Weeks · May 2022

This trip was a healing journey, taken about a year after I lost both my parents to COVID. It helped me work through a severe depression — to see light in immense darkness, and hope after living through so much loss. I met some extraordinary people along the way, and the whole experience gave me a real sense of perspective on life that carried me through a genuinely dark time.

→ Flew from Delhi to Leh on GoAir — at the time, there weren't many airlines flying this route. This is already a high-altitude place, and altitude sickness is a real risk — give your body at least 2 days to adjust before doing much. I learned this the hard way: I nearly fainted on day two, walking to the market thinking I'd already adjusted. There have been fatalities reported from altitude sickness here, so don't take it lightly.

Nubra Valley & Pangong Tso

1 Week

Drove through Khardung La Pass at around 18,000 feet — the wind chill and lack of oxygen at that altitude meant focusing on nothing but the moment just to get through it safely. Watch out for glacier or ice roads out here too — they're sometimes nearly invisible to the naked eye but genuinely dangerous and slippery; I almost got frostbite on my feet from being underprepared for how extreme the cold got. Stayed in tents booked through Booking.com along the way through the valley.

→ Rented a Royal Enfield Himalayan 400cc and rode across Nubra Valley, Pangong Tso Lake, Shyok, and Durbuk.
